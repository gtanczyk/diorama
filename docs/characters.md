# Characters, style and icons

## Characters

`build` also makes the characters, after the places (steps `cast`, `model`, `sprites`; each also runs alone):

- `diorama cast <slug>` paints the hero and every NPC (cast/<key>.png, with what they hold: a lantern or a net SAM's person leaves out stays on, gaps it leaves are filled, and what floats beside them as theirs, a heart or the bubble they are in, comes along) and makes their 2D walk: SAM finds arms, legs and boots to place the joints, then the whole painting is bent by those bones as one fine mesh, not cut into parts, so a coat, an apron or a skirt stretches with the legs and never tears (src/scene/warp.ts). The walk is drawn once into a sheet (cast/<key>/walk.png, 8 frames and the untouched painting for standing; joints in cast/<key>/rig.json) and measured: a walk that opens holes or loses a piece is marked in rig.json `check` and the game slides the painted figure as one piece instead. `diorama rig <slug> [key...]` (Studio agent: `cast_rewalk`) makes the walk again from the paintings already there, with no Muse calls. A drawing SAM does not see as a person is asked as a character; when it still finds none, `cast_status` says why in `rigIssue` and `moves` says how the game moves them instead. Walk sheets whose frames left the pose are listed in `spriteIssues`; `sprites_redo` paints new takes. Spark also says how tall each one stands and whether upright like a person (cast/figures.json, a grown-up 1.62 m): a cat or a child is drawn at its own size, and a creature that is not upright (an animal, a round eye on tiny feet, a living teapot) gets no 2D rig or sprites: it moves as one body, hopping as it goes, squashing as it lands, leaning the way it goes, breathing and blinking (src/play/creature.ts), as its painted figure or, when it is in the sprites plan, as its own 3D model.
- `diorama model <slug>` gives each character who needs sprites a rigged 3D model: Muse paints them from four sides, TRELLIS turns the views into a mesh and Make-It-Animatable adds a skeleton (both open Hugging Face Spaces; set `HUGGING_FACE_API_KEY` or `HF_TOKEN`, as they run on your account's GPU quota). The model only supplies poses. A creature (not upright) is painted from four sides as it is (no A-pose, no hands) and keeps TRELLIS's mesh with no skeleton (`model.json` `creature: true`): the game hops, squashes, leans and blinks the whole model, so a creature hero or NPC needs no sheets. Studio's Cast **Animate in 3D** (action `cast_animate`) builds a model for anyone the build skips, and the model step keeps building it. The figure is stood up straight in the file, and the poses start from the shape the skin was bound in (the reconstructed figure), not from whatever pose the rig saved its bones in (Make-It-Animatable's T-pose can sit a figure in a long coat down). What the character wears (a satchel, a backpack) is read off the front view and named in every other view's prompt with the side it shows on; Spark then checks all four together, and a view with it on the wrong side, or with anything standing on the ground at the feet, is painted again (up to 2 rounds; a wrong front repaints all four once). Things held in the hands are left out of the views: a suitcase put down beside the feet would be fused to a boot and swing with the leg in every sprite. A view the image safety filter refuses is asked once more in calm words (the looks left to the picture, the hands said plainly); refused again, the model made before stays, or the character walks with its 2D rig, and the build goes on.
- `diorama sprites <slug>` draws their walk and talk sheets (cast/<key>/sprites/): the model walks or gestures in a sheet of 8 frames, Muse draws the sheet again in the world's style, and each frame is cut out and lined up with its pose. A sheet that Muse handed back barely changed is drawn again over a different guide. A walk frame Muse painted out of the gait (a crouch, a sit: the painted figure under 88% of the sheet's usual height) is listed as `off` in sprites.json; the walk steps over it and standing still never shows it. Only what the story uses is drawn: the walk facings the hero's routes need, one talk sheet per speaker in the facing of their conversation, and the gesture Spark picks for each line (`--plan` prints the plan without drawing). Rendering the guides needs Playwright's Chromium (`npx playwright install chromium`).

The page draws the best a character has: sprite sheets, else the 2D walk, else the plain cut-out. The bare 3D model (TRELLIS's textures up close: a smeared face, melted hands) only supplies poses for the sprites; the page walks a person with it only with `?model=1` (a creature keeps its model).

## Speaking faces

`diorama faces <slug> [character...]` prepares reusable mouth shapes and subtle eyelid/brow movement for painted figures, their 2D rigs and visible talk-sprite poses. `build` runs `faces` after cast, sprites and voices. Studio's Cast card has **Prepare speech animation** / **Refresh speech animation**; the same job is `faces_prepare`. `faces_status` reports image/rig/sprite coverage, synchronized recording counts, skipped faces, failed preparation and the model's 3D facial capabilities.

The configured vision model locates the head, lower face and eyes. The image model paints nine mouth shapes for each new visible view; vision validates their identity and articulation and calibrates the cells individually. Rejections identify bad frame indices. Up to three bad frames get individual mouth edits while neighbouring cells remain unchanged; wider or unlocalized failures get a full retry with the diagnostic feedback. The complete atlas is validated again before use. There is only one repair round: one image request normally, at most four including repairs, plus vision calls. When F/V (upper teeth on the lower lip, which the image model keeps painting as a grin) is the only frame still failing after its repair, the B mouth (teeth nearly together) stands in for it with no further image request, and the rest of the atlas is used. Static patches are rejected. The mouth/eye registrations are bound to the source PNG, sprite sheet or rig data; repaints disable stale registrations. A failed preparation retains the previous assets. Back-facing, obscured and unrecognizable faces stay as painted and are reported. An unchanged run reuses its assets and model-call cache.

The actual lip corners in each source pose and the neutral atlas are registered separately. Each atlas frame is aligned separately by its lip centre and upper rim. An uncertain generated lip location gets one magnified lower-face inspection, mapped back into the original cell coordinates; confidence thresholds remain unchanged and unresolved frames retain the previous assets. Patches scale uniformly from that original mouth width and are masked to the lips; the nose, jaw outline and beard are preserved. Closed source poses use the original pixels at rest. A gesture painted with an open mouth (`sourceOpen`) first receives a narrowly masked neutral lip patch, so silence and M/B/P close its original cavity without moving the jaw silhouette. Uncalibrated mouths stay as painted. A single premultiplied crossfade avoids stacking two feathered faces. Painted figures use their original pixel coordinates. A 2D rig uses the actual painted silhouette to map the cropped figure into its padded canvas; baked walk cells follow the torso's rise/fall, and older part rigs keep the face on their torso. Each talk-sprite pose has its own registration. Mirroring, scale, lighting and occlusion are applied to the composed figure by the existing renderer.

The final, already-processed voice files are analysed locally with [Rhubarb Lip Sync](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync), cached by SHA-256. The first run finds Rhubarb on PATH or downloads its pinned official 1.14.0 release. Set `DIORAMA_RHUBARB=/path/to/rhubarb` or pass `--rhubarb PATH` to use an installed executable. FFmpeg is also required. English uses PocketSphinx; other languages, including Polish, use the approximate language-independent phonetic recognizer. `--audio-only` refreshes timelines without painting anything. Existing lip-sync is refreshed after Voices or a single voice retry. The recordings themselves are never modified.

The Web Audio source drives mouth timing, independent of simulation speed. Loading, interruption, completion, mute and audio-context suspension return the relaxed mouth. Blinking has a per-character phase. `?lipsync=0` disables facial animation for comparison.

3D rendering consumes the same performance state in two ways. Models with recognized authored facial morphs use ARKit-style jaw/blink/brow/funnel/pucker names and common VRM/Oculus visemes; unrelated morphs are left intact. Reconstructed models with projected painted views use independently calibrated front/left/right facial surfaces. Those canvases update the textures on the actual mesh, following its rotation, skinning, lighting and occlusion; they do not create a mouth cavity or change the jaw geometry. Each view owns its own painter, and unchanged frames avoid redundant GPU uploads. A back-facing head keeps its original paint. Native UV-textured models without facial morphs need facial rigging; the projected-surface fallback does not silently replace their material.

`faces_status.model3d` reports the active method (`morphs` or `painted-surfaces`), its supported views and native morph count. Source hashes cover the model view and its fit settings. `faces_status.spritePoses` separately counts registered, obscured/skipped and pending talk poses. In-progress atlases live in `face-candidate.json` and content-hashed PNGs; a failed calibration keeps the previous live face, and retry resumes the candidate without paying for the same image again. Every atlas mouth is located individually in its full head, cropped at one common scale, then calibrated again in the final crop and aligned by its lip centre and upper edge. Crops extending beyond a generated cell use transparent padding so background rectangles cannot paint over a beard. Source lips are located with the surrounding face visible, then reviewed in close-up. Sprite poses are inspected separately in a cropped upper-body region. Coordinate-analysis resizes explicitly preserve the full input; uncertain lip boxes are saved as proposals, never activated automatically. Partial successful registrations survive a failed pose.

The local review at `/prototype/conductor-lipsync` compares all four characters as an image, 2D rig, sprites or a 3D model in the actual game scene, including a model rotation control. Its close-up disables optional rim/separation and paint smoothing, hides the other hero when reviewing an NPC, and renders the selected 3D model at the enlarged display resolution for a sharp, smooth review. The normal game keeps its existing presentation. Mouth timing in Polish and the visual quality of newly generated faces still need review with the recordings.

## Changing the style

`diorama style <slug> "<style>"` (the Studio's Style button, the `style_set` action) paints a finished world in another style, say from a flat cartoon to a realistic oil painting. Each place is repainted from the painting it has, every fix in it, by one Muse edit that keeps the composition (camera, things, floors, doors where they are and as big), then cut out, measured and checked again from `masks` to `verify`, so the person's fixes (SAM phrases, boxes, brush strokes, floor cells, stand spots) keep lining up. Each character is repainted from their own figure, same face, clothes and pose, and their 3D model and sprite sheets are made again; the hero painted from a photo is painted from the photo again. Children's drawings go with each repaint as the reference, so the children still know their things. Sound and voices stay as they are.

`--place KEY` restyles one place and `--cast KEY` (or `--cast all`) the characters: the style is kept as that place's (`corrections.json`) or character's (`cast/fixes.json`) own, over world.json's; restyling the whole world clears them. `--estimate` only says what it redoes and about what it costs, from the calls this world's own build sent (the Studio and the agent always ask with it first). `styled.json` keeps the style each place and character was last painted in, which is how `paint` and `cast` know a picture is to be repainted rather than painted anew. In the Studio it runs as one job, so History takes it all back at once.

## Inventory icons

`diorama icons <slug> [item...]` (a build step, after `plates`) makes `items/<key>.png` for each item in `story.json`, and nothing for anything else. An item the hero picks up in a place is cut out of that place's painting with the thing's SAM mask, so it costs no model call and looks exactly like what the player saw lying there. One that is too small there to blow up (under 56 px) is redrawn larger by Muse from the cut-out; one that only a person hands over is painted by Muse in the world's style, then cut out by SAM (or, when SAM finds nothing, as what stands out from Muse's plain ground; the shaded wash it stands on is peeled off the edge). `items/items.json` says how each was made. To change one, use the Studio's Cast tab or the agent: `icon_redo` takes a hint in your words, `cut` or `paint`, or another take, and keeps it in `items/fixes.json` for every later build (`icon_fix_clear` takes it back; `icons_status` lists them all).

Speaking animation is an optional build stage: a reported face/audio preparation failure is saved as `faces.status: incomplete` with diagnostics, while other build steps continue. Explicit repair jobs still report failure. This does not mark missing voice recordings or unplayable places as ready. `face-preparation.json` tracks current per-character failures so successful targeted repairs clear their warnings without hiding other characters' failures.

Atlas work is checkpointed before calibration in `cast/<key>/face-work/`. An accepted atlas never gets repainted merely because a later lip detector is uncertain; successful lip detections also survive retry. `faces_status.repairWork` lists source-bound work, stage and failed mouths. `faces_inspect` shows an exact head cell and reference, with a revision token. `face_frame_repair` edits only one inspected cell and revalidates the entire atlas; `face_lips_set` records inspected lip corners in an accepted head cell. Both resume preparation and leave the live face unchanged until the remaining checks pass.

For later calibration stages, `face_calibration_inspect` lists pending source poses or final mouth patches and shows their exact close-up. `face_calibration_set` stores a reviewed normalized lip box and visible-cavity flag against both the candidate revision and inspected pixel hash. These corrections cannot be reused on a repaint or another crop. They do not bypass identity/articulation checks. The agent must inspect the image and cannot invent coordinates when the mouth is ambiguous. New tool definitions require restarting an already-running Studio server.
