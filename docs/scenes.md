# Places: depth, weather and the play page

## Depth: parallax and moving water

`diorama backdrop <slug>` (a build step, after `derive`) turns each place into a little stage, so it moves like a real diorama:

- **Backdrop and cards.** One Muse edit paints the place without its standing things; only their masks are taken from it, and SAM checks each thing is really gone (a leftover gets a smeared fill). The things become cut-out cards. In the page the floor shears with the camera, each card slides with the floor row it stands on, and the upright band behind the walkable floor (a quay wall, a railing) slides as one piece so it does not lean. The hero slides with the floor under his feet and still hides behind cards.
- **Depth occlusion.** Cards hide people only behind the things the story names. Everything else in the painting (a lamp post, a railing, a bridge's parapet) hides them by the depth map (`app/src/play/depth-occlusion.ts`): a pixel nearer than the feet by 12%+ of their floor row, whose column meets the floor in front of the feet, is put back over the person from the frame as drawn without them. A wall right behind someone (which the depth model reads a little near) never hides them; stairs from `steps` are a raised floor; more than 75% of a person hidden by it means the depth is unsure there, and it hides nothing. `verify` reports `NO_DEPTH` (no depth map, so unnamed things hide no one), `WALK_ON_FACE` (walk cells on an upright face such as a bridge's side) and `DEPTH_UNSURE`; the explorer counts a visible hero over a nearer painting as `drawn-over` (worst `depth`).
- **No paths up a wall.** When derive joins floors that do not touch, it opens the cheapest corridor of cells between them; that corridor first may not cross an upright surface (the side of a bridge, the wall down to an underpass), told from the depth model before the scale is known (`uprightTest` in `app/src/scene/depthmap.ts`: on a floor the depth changes steadily down the screen, on a wall it hardly does). Only a floor no other path reaches is joined across a wall as before, and derive flags it ("no floor joins … but up a wall"). Cells on such a face, joined or laid by a floor mask, are flagged `WALK_ON_FACE` by verify with their box: the fix is a floor or stair between the two (`floor_cells`, a floor brush), not the wall. A corridor along a one-cell fringe that can't be widened is laid again opening every cell it takes, instead of looping.
- **Whole cards.** A thing that stands nearer slides more than the wall behind it; that is the diorama. So a card must be the whole thing: SAM by name often takes a container and leaves what is in or on it (a bucket, not the fish heads over its rim), and that part would stay on the backdrop and slide apart. `masks` asks SAM once more by a box around the whole thing (the mask's box joined with Spark's, grown up and sideways) and joins the parts it finds attached to the mask; parts that reach the box's edge are asked by a bigger box. `backdrop` then checks every card's outline: where it cuts through something Muse took out with the thing, the card is flagged `CARD_CUT` (in `backdrop.json` and the verify flags), and the Studio agent fixes it with `mask_fix` and a box around the whole thing.
- **Far flats.** Spark names the bands of scenery above the horizon, farthest first (hills, a town, a castle); SAM cuts each one from the backdrop into `layers/<n>.png`, over a sky with the flats filled in (`layers/sky.png`). Each flat slides a little more than the one behind it.
- **Water.** SAM finds water on the backdrop (minus the floors and cards, which come out wet otherwise) into `layers/water.jpg` and `layers/water-mask.png`. Spark reads which way it runs on screen, along the channel and into the depth too; that is `water.flow` in `layers.json`, a unit vector downstream, `[0, 0]` for still water like a harbour basin or the sea, which the page sways in slow swells instead of running. Short light glints come and go on every water, drifting with it, so even a dark smooth sea is seen to move. If Spark got the river backwards, tell the Studio agent ("the canal flows toward the bridge"): its `water_flow` action keeps `reverse`, `still` or a hint in words in `places/<key>/depth.json`, which every later build follows.
- **Painted fog.** SAM finds fog, mist or haze painted on the backdrop (minus the cards; skipped when it is more than half the frame, which is the weather's mist) (when SAM sees no fog, which is often, Spark boxes the fog bands and the fog in them is read from the pixels: pale and greyed) into `layers/mist.png`, a veil of the fog's own colour shaped like its denser and lighter patches, and `layers/mist-mask.png`, where it may show. The page slides two copies of the veil sideways with the wind, cross-fading half a cycle apart, so wisps roll across the land while the land itself stays put; over walkable floors the veil is thin. To change it, tell the Studio agent ("the fog should drift left", "slower", "leave the fog still"): its `fog_drift` action keeps `fog` (auto, left, right, still) and `fogSpeed` in `places/<key>/depth.json` and reruns the backdrop step.

- **Living lights.** The lights Spark placed in the layout (`layout.lights`) come alive in the page, by the words of their names (`src/play/glow.ts`): a window's glow breathes, a lantern, candle or fire flickers, far town lights twinkle, and a lighthouse or beacon turns its beam round, swinging left and right and flaring when it points at us. They sit where the lights were painted, not on Spark's planned points: Muse paints a lamp where the picture wants it (a lighthouse lamp on its tower, well above the point the plan gave). `derive` (`src/scene/glows.ts`) moves each warm light to the strongest bright, warm spot near its point, a lighthouse lamp to the strongest high one anywhere in the upper half when none is near; a light with no painted spot keeps its point only if that is on a painted thing, else it gets no glow. The moved points are `geometry.glows`; a place derived before this keeps the planned points until `diorama derive <slug>` runs again (free: the model answers are remembered). Only light is added over the painting, never covering it. `?glow=0` keeps them as painted (capture mode too, unless `?glow=1`).

`--smear` skips Muse and smears the gaps instead. Places with a `photo.png` stay flat.

- **Separation.** Each person throws a soft shade on what stands just behind them: their silhouette blurred and pushed a little away from the light, only on pixels the depth map (from `derive`; without one, the floor plane with each card on its floor row) puts a short way past their feet. So a hero passing a painted plinth reads as standing in front of it, not pasted on it. `?separate=0` turns it off, `?separate=1.5` darker.
- **Rim light.** Each person gets a thin warm edge on the side the light comes from (against the way shadows fall; a place with no measured light gets none), as illustrators separate a figure from a busy background. `?rim=0` off, `?rim=1.5` wider.
- **Focus** (off by default). `?focus=1` softens and pales what is behind the hero with its depth, like a close-up camera. It only separates far things, so it stays opt-in.

## Weather: rain, snow and drips

`diorama weather <slug>` (a build step, after `backdrop`) reads what falls on each place and where water drips, into `places/<key>/weather.json`; the page animates it all, so nothing in the painting is a frozen streak:

- **Fall.** Spark looks at the painting and the place's mood: `rain`, `snow` or `none` (indoors, a cave, wet ground after the rain), how hard (0.3 drizzle, 1 a downpour) and how much the wind slants it. The page drops rain or snow over the whole frame in three depths; each drop ends on the floor row as far away as it is.
- **Rings and splashes.** SAM finds the water and the puddles (`weather/ripples.png`, minus the floors and cards SAM takes for water). Drops that land there ring; drops that land on a walkable floor splash; on a roof or the sky nothing shows.
- **Drips.** Spark names up to four edges that drip (eaves, a gutter, an awning, a pipe, a cave ceiling), SAM finds each, and its lowest edge is where drops swell and let go. Each falls under gravity, at the scale of the place, to the floor row under it (the depth map, else the first walkable cell below) and splashes or rings there. A dry place can still drip.
- **Painting.** When a place's words say rain or snow, the paint step asks Muse for the weather's marks only (wet sheen, puddles, snow lying), no streaks in the air.

To change it, tell the Studio agent ("no rain in the hut", "it should pour", "water drips from the tavern sign"): its `weather_set` action keeps `fall`, `strength`, `fog` (mist drifting across the place, 0..1, which Spark also reads from the picture and the mood) and a hint in words in `places/<key>/weather-wish.json`, which every later build follows. `?weather=0` in the play page keeps every place dry.

## In the play page

- **Walking:** `derive` joins the floors up, but never across painted water (SAM finds it only when a join would cross it), and the game starts on the biggest stretch of floor. The hero routes over the walk grid; the walk phase follows the distance walked, so feet don't slide. Things in front of a person (per-column floor lines from `derive`) are drawn back over them.
- **Stairs:** `derive` asks SAM for the steps of any floor or walk-on thing named as stairs (stair, step, schody) and keeps each step's band per column (`steps` in geometry.json). A person on them keeps their size from where they stand, but their feet sit on the tread under them and climb only over each riser; a walk never ends on a riser. Fewer than two clear steps keep the stairs a ramp.
- **Light and shadow:** `derive` measures the painting's light from the shadows things cast (`light` in geometry.json) and masks those shadows (`masks/shadows.png`). A person then casts a matching soft shadow and darkens inside a painted one. A shadow many times its thing's size (stair risers, a tree's shade) is not measured. When Spark sees sun but no thing's own shadow can be measured, the light comes from Spark and nothing is masked. A place with no sun (fog, overcast) gets neither. Each person has one shadow: under measured light the cast shadow replaces the soft blob at the feet (only a thin contact under the soles stays); in shade, fog or without light the blob is the shadow.
- **Shadows on the ground's shape:** `derive` also writes `depth.png` (`depth` in geometry.json): Depth Anything V2 Small (ONNX on the CPU, about a second per place; the 27 MB model is fetched once into `.diorama/models/`) gives relative depth, and a robust line through the walk cells pins it to the floor the scale already knows, as the floor row at the same distance. The runtime turns each ground pixel into a 3D point and asks whether the person stands between it and the sun (`app/src/play/shadow.ts`), so a shadow breaks over steps, climbs a wall and drops off a quay. It skips what the painting already shades and fades to almost nothing on water. When the depth fits less than 35% of the walk cells (or the model can't run) there is no `depth.png`, the place is flagged `depth: ...`, and shadows lie flat as before.
- **Fires and lamps:** where the layout has lights, `derive` asks Spark whether one of them (not the sky) lights the ground, and whether it is a living flame; it is stored as `light.at` (and `light.flame`). The layout often names a light by its glow ("lamp-glow", "oven-fire") rather than its thing; such a light goes to the thing near its point that shares a word with it, else the one whose mask holds the point, else the smallest box around it. Each person's shadow then runs straight away from it, fading a few heights away. With the depth map the painted things cast shadows from it too (a bench toward the viewer, a tent away from the fire): each floor point looks back along its ray to the light and is shaded where the ray passes behind a thing. A person's shadow that reaches a thing standing behind them (a tent wall) lies on it. A flame flickers: it sways about 10 cm and its light breathes, so every shadow wavers with it, its bright pixels dance, a warm glow pulses round it and a few sparks rise.
- **Inventory:** the items the hero carries sit in a paper bag at the bottom, each as its painted icon (step `icons`, below). Click one to take it in hand: it becomes the cursor, with "Use X on Y" over a thing; right click or Esc puts it back. Tried where it does not help, the hero says the item's own line (`nope` in story.json, written with the story). A picked-up item flies from where it lay in the painting into its slot, and the painting shows the spot empty (the `plates` step: Muse removes the thing, and only its mask, grown by any part Muse removed with it that SAM left out, such as a rope tied to it, is patched in, matched in tone; of several takes the one with the least seam and no stain is kept, and `plates.json` gives both).
- **Touch and keyboard:** on a phone or tablet a tap walks or uses, a tap on a slot takes the item in hand and another puts it back, and a long press looks at a thing (or puts the item back). WASD or the arrows walk the hero and slide along whatever is in the way; the nearest thing in reach lights up and E, Space or Enter uses it; 1 to 9 take an item from the bag.
- **Gamepad:** any pad works without keys or a mouse, so a small child can play. The stick or d-pad walks, A uses the lit thing (in a conversation it picks the lit answer, at the end it plays again), B puts the item in hand back or looks, X, Y and the shoulder buttons go through the bag one item at a time and back to empty hands. The 8BitDo Micro is read as a standard pad in its D-input mode and in the Mac's MFi profile (buttons by face position, the cross from the hat or the first axes), as the gamedev.pl site does. Other pads the browser does not map work roughly: the two lowest buttons use, the rest go through the bag. Several pads all steer the same hero, or with `?pads=turns` they take turns: a badge in the player's colour shows whose turn it is, and the turn passes to the next pad after a conversation ends or after 60 seconds of play (`&turn=90` for more), never in the middle of a line. With `?pads=party` every pad gets its own hero on screen, the hero's figure dressed and edged in the player's colour (clothes recoloured as the page loads; skin and head keep theirs), on a ring and under a number in that colour: each walks on its own, the bag and the story are shared, and whoever uses or looks at something becomes the hero the story follows (only that player answers in a conversation). When each child draws their own hero, mark the drawings "Another hero (one per gamepad)" in the Studio workshop (kind `player`): `cast` paints and rigs each one, and pad 2 plays the first of them, pad 3 the next (in a party each walks as drawn; in turns the hero turns into whoever plays). They join no place and say nothing in the story. A pad press does not unlock sound in every browser; while sound is held back the page asks for one click. In Studio, Play has a Gamepads choice (one hero, take turns, a hero each).
- **Talking:** each line knows who says it; the paper bubble sits over the speaker with their name on a tag, and the speaker plays the line's gesture while the other listens. The hero's answers are numbered cards beside his portrait; keys 1 to 4 pick them too.
- **URL switches:** `place=<key>` start there, `spawn=x,y,left|right` put the hero, `capture=1` fixed 1/60 s steps for recording (flat, no parallax), `parallax=N` parallax strength (`0` flat, default 1 with the mouse), `track=N` a closer camera that pans after the hero, for walks along a place, `sway=1` a slow idle drift for films, `separate=N` the shade a person throws on what is just behind them (`0` off, default 1; off in capture runs unless given), `rim=N` the light edge on each person (`0` off, default 1; off in capture runs unless given), `focus=N` soften what is far behind the hero (default 0), `still` water that does not flow, `grid=1` show the walk grid and occluders, `model=1|0` prefer or skip the 3D figure, `sound=0` silent, `cursor=0` hide the cursor. `window.diorama` drives the page from scripts: `walkTo`, `use`, `act`, `play(steps)`, `wait`, `on(event)`.

## Checking a world

`diorama build` ends with `verify`, which writes `verify.json` per place and prints which places are playable and what was flagged on the way. Places are built side by side and independently, and no place waits for another: each goes through its own steps and is told playable (`place.verdict`) as soon as its verify and free repairs are done, while the rest are still being made. The world's own steps start as soon as what they read is there (`app/src/world/schedule.ts`): the cast is painted from world.json while the places are, voices once the story is, an item's icon once its place is cut out, sprites once every place has its walking routes, sound once every place is painted. A place whose step breaks is set aside with why, and the others go on to the end, characters and sound included. Each model has a cap on requests open at once so the overlap does not run into rate limits (`DIORAMA_LANES="muse=8,spark=12,sam=16"`, the defaults). The report gives each error its playbook (`app/src/scene/playbook.ts`): where to look, the fix tools to try in order, and the costly last resort (a camera too close, `SCALE_NEAR` or a floor cut off once derive shut the front rows for it, is rerun derive first, then relayout and repaint); `place_status` hands the same to the Studio agent, which repairs one place at a time with the fix tools. The `masks` step also checks that each mask holds its thing: one Spark look per place at the painting with every mask box numbered (`app/src/scene/maskcheck.ts`), since a box and its mask can agree and both sit on the neighbour. When Spark points to where the thing really is, that is `MASK_WRONG` in verify (an error for any thing, props too). When it finds the thing nowhere and what it sees in the box is another kind of thing (hay where a horse should be), the thing was never painted: `THING_NOT_PAINTED`, an error whose play is to paint it in. A doubt about details only (a bronze bell for a brass one) is the flag `MASK_DOUBT`. Spark is asked to judge the kind of thing, not its details, and a box someone fixed is taken as given. Before anyone is asked, the build makes the cheap fixes itself (`app/src/scene/autofix.ts`): a gap of up to three 60 px cells with nothing solid in it, or up to six along stairs or a gangway (a walk-on thing) whose mask reaches both floors, is bridged with a floor brush stroke (two passes, as one bridge can bring the next patch within reach; stairs that lead somewhere else, with water between the floors, bridge nothing: that place needs a floor in the layout or a repaint, so it goes to the agent), a stand spot off the floor moves to the nearest reachable point (both without a model call), and a `MASK_WRONG` thing gets the box Spark saw it in (one SAM call). A card flagged `CARD_CUT` gets the cut part brushed onto its mask (a lantern on a boat's gunwale), so the card carries it and the backdrop is made again without it (once per cut). A `THING_NOT_PAINTED` thing left after them gets one Muse edit at its layout box (once per thing, kept as a repaint correction) and the place is rebuilt from paint. They are ordinary corrections, so `corrections_get` lists them and `corrections_clear` takes them back; `build.json` keeps them under `repairs`, and `--repair off` skips them. What the free fixes leave, the Studio hands to the agent at once: a build that ends with places unplayable wakes it with the places and their errors (`budget_set autoRepair:false` turns that off). It repairs with the fix tools in the playbook's order; after three fixes of a place in a row that leave it unplayable the fix tools refuse it and it tells you what it tried, which is your turn. `place_view` is how the agent looks, as a picture it sees (layer `walk`, `masks` or `painting`, all on the 60 px grid), so it has no need to dig through files; after three shell calls in a turn it is reminded of that once. Layer `masks` tints each thing's mask against the box it should be in and flags a mask that cut out a neighbour. Layer `walk` shows why a place cannot be walked: reached floor green, unreached red, each gap as a dashed line naming the 60 px cells to bridge (the ones `floor_cells` takes) and the things in it, and stand spots off the floor (`places/<place>/diagnose.png`; the agent gets it as an image). Every step also leaves a picture to look at: `masks/overlay.png` (what SAM found) and `walk.png` (where the hero can walk, footprints, floor lines and his size at a few depths).

`diorama explore <slug>` puts the hero on every part of each floor in the real play page and checks each frame; it needs Playwright with Chromium (`diorama browsers` downloads it once). `--fix` closes the floor where he looked wrong.


## Scene reactions

The `reactions` build step runs after weather and waits for the story. The configured Spark planner reads the painting, floor descriptions, geometry, weather, world style and available story interactions. It chooses restrained dust, splashes, snow, leaves or sparks only where the material and action support them; clean surfaces can have no effects. No model or audio is needed while playing.

`reactions-plan.json` keeps the planner's choices. The compiler binds floor keys and optional local polygons to reachable cells of the actual walk/zone grid; it rejects unknown floors, empty areas, overlapping step recipes, unknown conditions and unreachable interaction targets. `reactions.json` is the validated runtime data. A fingerprint disables stale reactions after the scene, story or author preference changes. Rerun `reactions <slug> [place...]` to update an older world; ordinary builds and restyles include the step automatically.

- **Step:** fires at half-cycle crossings during actual movement, including the last step before stopping. Uses the local material, depth scale and stair height. The foot position is approximate for sprite sheets.
- **Enter:** fires when a moving actor crosses into the region, including a small patch crossed within one frame. Spawning or standing inside does not fire. Optional story conditions, cooldowns and once-per-play-session entry prevent repetition.
- **Interaction:** fires for the exact matched use/take story action with the hero in reach. Looking, an unmatched action, an old action after a story edit, and a different refusal branch cannot trigger that recipe. This is a reaction at the object, not a new hand-contact animation.

Particles use seeded randomness, a shared limit of 96, short lifetimes, the scene camera and object occlusion. They clear on place entry; reduced-motion preference suppresses particles. `?reactions=0` disables them for an A/B comparison. Export embeds the validated rules and bundles the renderer without new image assets.

The Studio agent has `reactions_status` and `reactions_set` (a natural-language hint, saved in `reactions-wish.json` across rebuilds). After changing reactions it reloads Play. Verify event-driven motion by walking through the region or using the object; an idle screenshot cannot demonstrate it.
