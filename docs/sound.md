# Sound and voices

`diorama sound <slug>` (also a build step) has Spark plan what the world sounds like from its places, the brief of each painting and the story: a music bed, the ambience of every place, loops on things that sound (a lantern flame, a flag in the wind, a lamp that only roars once the story lights it), the surface of every floor, and one-shots for taking, using, exits, talking and the ending. The plan is `sound.json`; edit it and run the step again to regenerate only what changed.

The Meta Model API has no audio model, so effects come from ElevenLabs (`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`) and music from Meta's open-weights MusicGen run on your machine (`DIORAMA_PYTHON=/path/to/python` with `torch transformers scipy`; without it, or with `--music eleven`, ElevenLabs makes the music). Code checks every take: a loop that came back as one event is asked for again, loops are crossfaded into themselves so they never click, footstep takes are cut into single steps, and everything is levelled. Needs `ffmpeg`. Without a key the world plays silent.

`diorama voices <slug>` (also a build step) speaks the conversations, the intro and the endings (`--all` also voices the hero's remarks on things; by default those stay in the bubble so the game does not talk at every click). Spark looks at each figure and says who speaks (gender, age, how they sound). Adults get the fitting stock ElevenLabs voice; children, teens and the old get a voice made for them by **ElevenLabs Voice Design**, since no stock voice is a child or truly old (`voices.json`; a designed voice is created on the account only while its lines are read and deleted after, so a world needs one free voice slot; Studio's Cast card or `voice_set` recasts), ElevenLabs reads each line, and **Muse Voice Transcribe** listens to every take: a take that does not say its line is read again, up to three times, and rejected if it still does not.

**Quality and cost.** Final quality is the default: Multilingual v2 speech, up to 3 takes, 20-second ambience and 10-second object loops. `--draft` or `DIORAMA_SOUND=draft` opts into Flash v2.5, up to 2 takes, 10-second ambience and 6-second loops. `--final` or `DIORAMA_SOUND=final` explicitly selects final. These settings affect the next generation; existing recordings need the relevant `sound` or `voices` step rerun. Final generation can use more ElevenLabs credits. The credit floor remains `DIORAMA_ELEVEN_FLOOR` (2000 by default).

**Casting and dialogue.** Voice candidates prefer the story language; designed-voice reuse requires the same language, age, gender and performance brief. Old casts are migrated on the next voices run. Preview text comes from the character's dialogue, including for short non-English samples. Preview selection checks transcription and audio integrity, not highest pitch. Stock fallbacks are reported and are not artificially pitch-shifted, and only an adult gets one: a child, teen or old speaker whose voice Voice Design refused or could not check waits (voice `pending-design`, lines unspoken) until the next voices run or `voice_set` designs it again. A transcriber that does not answer (a busy queue, a dropped socket) is retried with a backoff and never counts against a voice; the paid previews are kept until it answers, so the next run checks them without designing again. Dialogue requests carry unambiguous preceding/following lines through the API context fields. Every speaker has their own recording even when two characters say the same text. New stories also pass an editorial review for natural phrasing, distinct personalities and speaker perspective, in addition to mechanical playability.

**Validation and reuse.** Paid responses remain in the memo. Shared effects require an exact prompt match and sufficient quality; a generic ID such as `shot-ending` cannot substitute another world's finale. Older assets are revalidated on rebuild. Silence, clipping, unusable steps, persistently noisy/noncontinuous loops and failed speech transcripts are rejected. A line with numbers or sums ("10+10=20") is read as words in the story language and checked as said; a take that says another number fails. Crickets, birdsong and other bright tones are not hiss: only noise without a pitch is. A rejected take is forgotten everywhere it was cached, so the next run asks for new takes, and a second take after a nearly silent one asks for it audible, not quieter. `sound_status` lists every sound and why one stayed silent; `sound_redo` (`diorama sound <slug> --redo <id,id>`) makes only those again, with a hint kept in `sound/fixes.json`. A partial result records `failures` in `sound/sounds.json` or `voices.json` and the command fails visibly. Files already generated are retained on disk; rejected recordings are not published in the manifest. Cast cards show unavailable lines and casting warnings. Technical checks do not establish that acting, timbre or artistic sound design is good: audition representative lines and gameplay audio before release.

Studio Setup → Audio → Audio quality saves Final or Draft for future generations in all worlds and CLI commands (`~/.config/diorama/sound.json`). CLI flags override `DIORAMA_SOUND`, which overrides the saved choice. Setup shows and locks the selector when the environment overrides it. A running sound or voices step keeps the quality it started with.

**Transcription outages.** Voice verification sends audio no faster than real time, with one active transcription per process. A busy or disconnected service gets at most four attempts with cooldowns and slower transmission (down to quarter speed); the timeout includes the recording's transmission time. A closed or failed stream stops sending immediately. Voice Design does not add another retry loop. Paid previews remain saved until verification succeeds, and a retry resumes pending voices without recasting working actors. Studio groups blocked lines by speaker and distinguishes pending verification, pending voice creation, rejected audio and generation failures. Its retry button reruns only Voices when no effects are missing.

To rebuild an existing world's audio: `diorama voices <slug>` and `diorama sound <slug>`. Rewriting the script is a separate `story` step; changing voice quality alone does not rewrite existing dialogue.

For one missing line, use `voice_retry` with the speaker key and exact text from `cast_status.voiceFailures`, or `diorama voice-retry <slug> <speaker> "<exact text>"`. Studio's retry button selects this operation when only one line is missing. It keeps the selected voice and every other recording. After a failed audio-quality check, both single-line and full voices retries use a new persisted seed batch rather than replaying the same rejected cache entries. If transcription is unavailable, the next retry checks that batch's saved audio instead of paying for another one. Original cached recordings are retained.

In the page, loops pan and fade with where the hero stands, footsteps follow the floor under him, and `m` mutes. `diorama playthrough --video` records the page's sound too and writes an mp4.
